Puck Around is a hobby project, maintained in spare time. Bug reports and
suggestions are welcome — the fastest route is
[a GitHub issue](https://github.com/vlumi/puckaround/issues), or email
[ville@misaki.fi](mailto:ville@misaki.fi).

## The basics

Lay the device flat between you. Each player **grabs their own mallet** — the
ring in their color — with a finger and keeps it; the mallet follows your
finger, kisses the center line, and never crosses it. During the faceoff,
grabbing your mallet is how you ready up; the moment everyone has, the force
field around the puck bursts and play begins.

The **center ring is the menu**: tap it any time for resume, restart, a new
match, or the way out. A drag *through* the ring is ordinary play — only a
clean tap opens it.

## Tournaments

The Tournament button runs an evening for any number of friends: **winner
stays** (the classic line by the couch), a **knockout bracket** for up to 32,
or a **league season** where everyone plays everyone. Names are typed once,
ever — after that they're one-tap chips, each with its own colors, worn by
the mallet and goal during play. Quitting the app keeps the evening; the
Tournament button resumes it.

## The arcade

The solo cabinets keep a top-ten board each. A run that makes the board asks
for a name at the next faceoff — tap a chip to sign, or skip. Boards can be
reset in Settings (the gear on the title screen): tap the reset once to arm
it, again to fire.

## No sound?

Puck Around respects the **silent switch** and mixes with your music rather
than ducking it. If the table is quiet: check the switch, then the speaker
icon in the pause menu (tap the center ring during play), then Settings →
Sounds.

## The puck teleported to the middle

That's the rescue beam, and it's deliberate: in the solo modes, a puck that
dies where nobody can ever reach it beams back to the serve — a ghost ring
where it stopped, a flare where it lands — instead of stalling the game. No
points change hands.

## Rotating the device mid-game

The board itself never turns relative to the device — your goal stays at your
end no matter how the device is held — while the labels and menus turn to
face the players. The system's rotation animation briefly spins the whole
screen; that's iOS, not the table moving.
